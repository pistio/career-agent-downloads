# The first-time GP locum guide

Practical guidance for the first months of freelance GP locum work in the UK.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is general information, not legal, tax, pension, financial or clinical advice. Rules and rates change.
> Check the linked official guidance before relying on it, and consider an accountant who understands GP work.
> The pension and performers-list sections focus on England; arrangements differ in Wales, Scotland and Northern
> Ireland. Links and guidance were checked in October 2026.

## 1. Understand the trade-off

Locum work can give you more control over where and when you work. In return, you take responsibility for tasks a
salaried post often handles in the background: finding and confirming work, keeping records, invoicing, chasing late
payments, setting aside tax, making pension submissions, arranging time off and maintaining appraisal evidence.

There is no holiday or sick pay built into a freelance session fee. Compare rates only after allowing for unpaid leave,
administration time, professional costs, pension choices and gaps between bookings.

## 2. Prepare before your first session

Start early, because some steps can take weeks.

- **Registration and licence:** make sure your GMC registration, licence to practise and GP Register status are correct.
- **Performers List:** for NHS GP work in England, check your status on the
  [National Performers List](https://pcse.england.nhs.uk/services/performers-lists/gp-performers). The other UK nations
  operate their own arrangements.
- **Indemnity and medico-legal support:** confirm what the state-backed scheme covers and keep suitable medical defence
  organisation support for matters outside that cover, including advice, complaints, inquests and private work.
- **Tax:** check whether and when you need to
  [register for Self Assessment](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment).
- **Banking:** a separate business account is not normally required for a sole trader, but it makes income and payment
  records much easier to reconcile.
- **Professional advice:** consider an accountant familiar with GP income and the NHS Pension Scheme.
- **Access:** arrange any NHSmail, smartcard and local system access you will need.
- **Peer support:** local locum groups, Local Medical Committees, professional associations and sessional GP networks
  can help with local rates, work and rule changes.

Keep a secure document pack ready for practices. It may include your CV, references, registration and performers-list
evidence, indemnity evidence, DBS information, occupational-health evidence and current training certificates. Share
only what the practice genuinely needs, through an appropriate channel.

## 3. Find work and agree clear terms

Work may come directly from practices, through local groups or chambers, or through a platform or agency. The route can
affect fees, contractual terms and whether work can be pensioned. Do not assume that every intermediary is treated the
same: check the current NHS pension rules for the particular arrangement.

A “session” is not a standard unit. Before accepting, agree in writing:

- the start and finish time;
- the number and length of appointments;
- whether visits, telephone work, prescriptions, results, letters or duty-doctor work are included;
- what handover and follow-up the practice expects;
- how extra work or overruns will be authorised and paid;
- the fee, and whether any employer pension contribution is additional to it;
- cancellation terms;
- when and where to invoice, the payment deadline and whether a purchase-order number is needed;
- who will approve any pension form or online submission.

Use written terms for each practice and confirm every booking by email or another durable written record. Model terms
from a professional association or locum organisation can be a useful starting point, but adapt them to the actual
work.

## 4. Keep reliable booking records

For each booking, record the practice, date, times, agreed fee, scope of work and any special terms. Put it in one diary
and check new dates for clashes or duplicates.

Keep the written confirmation. If a practice cancels, record the cancellation and retain the message. If your agreed
terms provide a cancellation fee, document the amount and why it is due.

A calendar or spreadsheet of completed sessions also becomes useful evidence for accounts, pension submissions and
appraisal.

## 5. Get oriented on the day

Allow extra time before a first session at a practice. Before the first patient, establish:

- how to access the clinical system, prescribing, referrals and printers;
- the practice's key contacts, local pathways, formulary and safeguarding leads;
- how to summon urgent help and where emergency equipment is kept;
- how results, tasks, letters, visits and prescriptions are handled;
- who can answer clinical or operational questions;
- what must be completed before you leave and how to hand over outstanding work.

Keep to the agreed scope. If extra work is requested, agree how it will be handled and paid. Make a clear written
handover of anything unfinished.

Never put patient-identifiable information in a personal diary, invoice, learning log or general administration tool.

## 6. Invoice promptly and follow up

Send invoices on the agreed schedule. Include a unique sequential invoice number, your business and payment details,
the practice's billing details, each work date and session, the agreed fees, any authorised extras, the payment deadline
and any required purchase-order reference.

Keep issued invoices unchanged. Correct a material mistake with an appropriate credit note and replacement invoice,
following your accountant's advice. Keep invoices, credit notes, payment records and relevant correspondence together.

When an invoice is overdue:

1. Check that the practice received it and that it has everything needed for payment.
2. Send a concise reminder quoting the invoice number, amount and due date.
3. Contact the practice manager or finance team if it remains unpaid.
4. Use a formal letter before taking recovery action, and get advice before adding interest or compensation.

The UK government explains the rules on
[late commercial payments](https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery).

## 7. Handle NHS pension work carefully

Freelance GP locums can choose whether to pension eligible NHS work. The choice affects cash flow, paperwork and future
benefits, so take advice that reflects your wider income and retirement plans.

For England, current official guidance says:

- freelance locum pensionable pay is 90% of the fee, excluding the employer contribution element;
- Locum A and B submissions are subject to a strict 10-week rule;
- employee and employer contributions must reach PCSE no later than the seventh day of the following month;
- the contribution tier depends on pensionable income and must be reviewed as income changes.

Use the current scheme-year forms and instructions. Submit promptly, keep evidence of submission and approval, and
check that contributions appear on your pension record. The clock continues while approval is outstanding.

Start with [PCSE's Locum A and B guidance](https://pcse.england.nhs.uk/services/gp-pensions/locum-gps/submit-locum-b-forms)
and the [current NHSBSA practitioner forms](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/member-hub/information-practitioners-and-non-gp-providers).
Do not rely on an old rate, form or saved deadline calculation.

## 8. Plan for tax and time off

Self-employed fees normally arrive without tax deducted. Put aside part of each payment in a separate savings account;
ask your accountant what proportion is appropriate for your circumstances.

Keep complete records of income and allowable business expenses. From the 2024–25 tax year, the cash basis is the
default for many sole traders, so income is generally recorded when received unless you opt out or cannot use it. HMRC
requires self-employed business records to be kept for at least five years after the relevant 31 January submission
deadline.

Official starting points:

- [Self Assessment registration](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment)
- [Self-employed business records](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-records)
- [Allowable expenses](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed)
- [Payments on account](https://www.gov.uk/understanding-your-self-assessment-tax-bill/payments-on-account)
- [Making Tax Digital for Income Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)

Budget for annual leave, illness, study, appraisal, administration and quiet periods. Consider independent advice about
income protection rather than assuming the session rate covers every risk.

## 9. Collect appraisal evidence

Maintain evidence across your whole scope of practice rather than reconstructing it at appraisal time:

- practices and settings where you worked, with dates and session counts;
- CPD and reflections;
- quality-improvement activity and significant events;
- colleague and patient feedback when required;
- concerns, restrictions or other information that must be declared.

The GMC requires doctors to tell their responsible officer about all organisations and settings in which they provide
medical services, including locum and self-employed work. Keep clinical reflections free of patient-identifiable
details.

See the GMC's [revalidation guidance](https://www.gmc-uk.org/registration-and-licensing/managing-your-registration/revalidation).

## 10. Protect your wellbeing

Working in different teams can be isolating. Leave space between clinical commitments for administration, CPD and
rest. Stay connected through peer groups and local professional networks. If you are struggling, seek help early;
[NHS Practitioner Health](https://www.practitionerhealth.nhs.uk/) offers confidential support for doctors and dentists
in England, and professional organisations and medical defence organisations also provide support.

## 11. Use a simple routine

| When | Action |
| --- | --- |
| When a booking arrives | Confirm the scope, fee and terms in writing; add it to one diary |
| After each session | Record completion, authorised extra time and any written handover |
| Weekly | Reconcile payments, chase overdue invoices and clear administration |
| At the agreed interval | Issue invoices and retain the supporting records |
| Each pension cycle | Submit the required forms and payment early; record approval |
| Every few months | Compare your records with your pension record and accounts |
| When rules change | Check current official forms, rates and deadlines |
| Before appraisal | Export your work record and update reflections and evidence |
| After the tax year | Give your accountant a complete income-and-expense pack |
